Product design specification · v1.0

CareHouse One

A multi-tenant platform for organizations that run group homes and residential care. One account covers every facility: residents, medications, staff, infection control, compliance and reporting, from a single home up to the whole organization.

Audience Product, design, engineering, compliance Platforms Web (desktop, tablet), iOS, Android Compliance target HIPAA, state licensing surveys, WCAG 2.2 AA Sample tenant Harborview Community Living, 5 homes, 42 licensed beds (fictional)
Foundations

Product principles

CareHouse One serves two very different users. Direct care staff need to finish a med pass with gloves on and a resident waiting. Executives need to see five homes at once and know which one needs a phone call. Every screen is judged against these six rules.

Three taps to chart

Logging a med, a note, a meal or a vital takes no more than three taps from any screen. The quick-action button is always available, and floor-staff screens open on what is due now.

Status never relies on color

Every state pairs color with a shape and a word: circle for done, diamond for pending, triangle for urgent. Isolation types use a letter and a distinct border style, so they still read in grayscale and for color-blind staff.

Scope is always visible

The scope switcher in the top bar always shows whether you are looking at one home or the whole organization. Numbers never change scope without saying so.

Safety stops are hard stops

Allergies, wrong-resident scans, duplicate doses and expired isolation reviews block the action until someone resolves them. Everything else warns and lets the user move on.

Every action leaves a trail

Views, edits, exports and overrides are recorded with who, when, where and why. The trail is searchable by surveyors and never editable by anyone.

Calm by default

Soft blues, greens and warm neutrals keep the screen quiet. Saturated color is reserved for things that need action, so it means something when it appears.

Structure

Tenancy, hierarchy & navigation

Each client organization is a separate tenant. Facilities, residents, staff and documents always belong to exactly one tenant, and nothing is shared across tenants. Inside a tenant, data rolls up from rooms to facilities to the organization.

Scope switcher

The first control in the top bar. It lists All facilities followed by each facility the user can access, with a live badge for an outbreak or overdue item. Choosing a facility filters every screen. Choosing All shows combined data, and every list adds a Facility column. Users assigned to one facility never see the switcher. Keyboard: Ctrl/⌘ + Shift + F.

Desktop left sidebar

Organization (org scope only) · Dashboard · Residents · eMAR · Daily Living · Staff & Schedules · Infection Control · Incidents · Reports · Templates (org admins) · Documents · Messages · Settings. Items the role can't use are hidden, not greyed out. The sidebar collapses to icons below 1280px.

Mobile bottom navigation

Five tabs: Today · Residents · eMAR · Tasks · More. Tabs show counts of items due. Clinical leads see Clinical in place of Tasks. Executives get Dashboard · Facilities · Alerts · Reports · More.

Quick-action button

A floating button, bottom right, opens a speed dial: Log Med, Add Note, Report Incident, Start Isolation, Screen Person, Clock In/Out. Actions depend on role. When opened from a resident's page, the resident is filled in automatically.

Each type has its own letter, hue and border style (solid, dashed, double, dotted). Badges appear on resident cards, room tiles, task rows, the eMAR, and the room board at the nurses' station. Combined orders stack, for example AC.

Typography

Lexend for headings and numbers

Atkinson Hyperlegible for body text and data entry. It was designed for low-vision readers and keeps letters like I, l and 1 apart, which matters in med names and doses.

IBM Plex Mono · MRN 0041-2287 · Lot 4H22B · 08:00

Scale: 12 / 14 / 16 (base) / 18 / 22 / 28 / 36. Floor-staff mode raises the base to 18px with 48px touch targets. Dose amounts never go below 16px.

Components & accessibility

  • Cards: 12px radius, 1px border, shadow only on floating layers (dialogs, quick-action button, toasts).
  • Contrast: text at least 4.5:1 and status icons at least 3:1 in both themes, checked in CI.
  • Touch targets of 44px or more, 48px in floor mode. Swipe actions always have a button alternative.
  • Full keyboard support, visible focus rings, screen-reader labels such as "Late, 41 minutes, Levetiracetam".
  • Dark mode follows the device setting, with a manual override. Night-shift mode dims further and warms the whites.
  • Reduced motion: no animated transitions, and alerts use a static banner in place of a pulse.

Layout zones

  1. Scope switcher. Set to All facilities. Choosing one home changes this view into that home's Facility Dashboard.
  2. KPI strip. Six tiles, each with a comparison line underneath. A tile that crosses a threshold gets a red left edge and a triangle pill, and clicking it opens the filtered list behind the number.
  3. Facility status board. One row per home, sorted by risk. Switch between table, card grid and map views. Clicking a row enters that facility's scope.
  4. Needs attention feed. Ranked by severity, then age. Each item shows its owner and due time. Acknowledge, assign or open from the row.
  5. Compliance heat map. Facilities against compliance areas. Each cell shows its number and links to the failing items.
  6. Trend panel. Twelve weeks of the selected metric with the latest point highlighted. A toggle overlays each facility as a separate line.

Behavior

  • Live updates arrive by websocket. A changed number flashes softly once (no flash with reduced motion).
  • Executives can pin up to 4 extra KPI tiles from the library, such as overtime hours, hospital transfers or bed-days lost.
  • Organization-wide numbers never include tenants other than the user's own. The scope label is printed on every export.
  • Tablet: KPI tiles wrap 3 by 2 and the board switches to cards. Phone: KPI tiles become a horizontal carousel, the attention feed comes first, and the board becomes a list of facility cards.

6+Quick action

Layout zones

  1. Situation banner. Shown only during an outbreak, a facility lockdown or a missed survey deadline. Stays at the top on every screen in this facility until the condition clears.
  2. Shift KPIs. Scoped to the current shift: census, staffing, the current med pass, care tasks and screening.
  3. Room map. Drawn in the onboarding floor-plan builder. Tiles show occupant, isolation badge and the most urgent status. Tap a tile for a resident quick view. Drag a resident to another room to transfer, which asks for confirmation.
  4. Due next. The facility's combined queue of meds, reviews, follow-ups and cleaning, ordered by deadline.
  5. Handoff note. The latest note from the outgoing shift. Each incoming staff member confirms they read it, and the read count shows here.
  6. Quick action. Opens the speed dial. Start Isolation and Report Incident are pinned during an outbreak.

Behavior

  • A Room Board display mode turns the map into a full-screen wall view for a station TV. It shows initials only, so no full names appear on a public screen.
  • Multi-floor homes get floor tabs above the map. Room tiles can be colored by isolation, acuity, fall risk or task status.
  • Managers can add widgets from a library: census forecast, open work orders, upcoming appointments, family messages.
  • Phone: the banner shrinks to a pill, KPIs become a carousel, the room map becomes a scrollable list grouped by isolation status, and Due next comes first.

Layout zones

  1. Identity header. Photo, name, pronouns, room, MRN, acuity, guardian and code status. The isolation badge and review date sit next to the name. The header stays at the top while scrolling.
  2. Safety strip. Allergies and diet texture always show and can't be collapsed. The same strip appears on every med and meal screen for this resident.
  3. Risk flags. Generated from assessments (fall, choking, elopement, skin) and linked to the protocol that applies. Each flag shows its source assessment and when it was last reviewed.
  4. Today timeline. Meds, care tasks, appointments and therapies in time order with status, all chartable from here.
  5. Vitals and goals. Trends drawn against the care plan's target range, plus progress toward personal goals.

Tabs

  • Care plan: goals, interventions and responsible roles, built from org templates. Versioned, with e-signatures from the resident or guardian and the clinician.
  • eMAR: this resident's MAR grid, orders, allergies, and pharmacy refill status.
  • Daily living: ADL records for hygiene, meals with percent eaten, mobility and toileting, plus activity attendance.
  • Notes: progress, behavior and handoff notes. Filter by author role or tag. Smart phrases speed up common entries.
  • Infection: case history, isolation orders, vaccinations and immunity, lab results.
  • Documents: intake packet, consents, assessments, ID scans and guardianship papers, each with an expiry date and a family-portal visibility setting.
  • Phone: the header shrinks to name, room and badges. The safety strip stays. Tabs become a scrollable chip row, and "Today" is the default tab.

Layout zones

  1. Resident rail. Everyone in the selected pass with progress counts and isolation badges. Isolated residents can be sorted last so the cart visits them at the end of the pass.
  2. MAR grid. Medications against administration times. The current pass column is highlighted. Each cell shows the time given and staff initials, or why a dose was held or refused. PRN and controlled meds get their own rows showing the effectiveness clock, the next allowed dose, and the count.

Phone med pass (left phone)

  • Scan the wristband or the room QR, then a face-photo check. A wrong-resident scan is a hard stop.
  • Each med is scanned from its blister pack. A wrong drug, wrong dose, duplicate dose or allergy match is a hard stop that needs an RN override with a reason.
  • Vitals required by the order, such as BP before an antihypertensive, are asked for inline. Values outside the order's limits suggest a hold.
  • Swipe left on a med to mark Refused, Held or Not available, with a reason picker and an automatic RN notification.

Rules & integrations

  • Time windows: set per facility, defaulting to 60 minutes either side. Doses become Due at the window start and Late at its end, and a missed-dose alert goes to the shift lead after 30 minutes and the RN after 60.
  • Controlled substances: a witness (a second user) signs on the same device with their own PIN. Shift-change counts are blind double counts, and any discrepancy creates an incident automatically.
  • Double-check: configurable per drug class, such as insulin and anticoagulants.
  • PRN: requires a reason. The effectiveness check is scheduled automatically (default 60 min). Max-dose and minimum-interval limits are enforced.
  • Pharmacy: orders arrive by e-prescribing or an HL7/FHIR pharmacy feed, and an RN must verify them before they appear on the MAR. Refill requests trigger at 7 days on hand. Deliveries are checked in by scan.
  • Quick action (right phone): the speed dial is open over the Today screen. It gives one-tap access from any screen, and the backdrop dims everything else.

Layout zones

  1. Scope and sub-tabs. Organization-wide by default for the infection preventionist and facility-scoped for everyone else. Sub-tabs cover the whole program: surveillance, outbreaks, screening, audits, PPE and vaccinations.
  2. Case board. Cases move from Suspected to Confirmed to Recovering to Cleared. Each card shows isolation badges, the review clock and lab status. The line list view gives the same data as a sortable table that can be exported for public health.
  3. Epi curve. Cases by onset date, residents stacked with staff, with the outbreak declaration marked. Filter by facility, organism or symptom group.
  4. PPE inventory. Days of supply calculated from recent use. Alerts fire below the reorder point, and inventory can be moved between facilities in a few taps.
  5. Outbreak checklist. The steps of the declaration workflow with who did what and when, plus the projected close date.

Isolation order behavior

  • Starting an order (precaution type, reason, start time, review interval) adds the badge everywhere the resident appears, posts PPE steps to every task for that resident, and adds a door-sign printout to the queue.
  • Review clocks count down on the resident header. When a review is overdue, the order turns red and the RN and infection preventionist are alerted until it is extended or discontinued.
  • Contact tracing: the system builds a 72-hour exposure list from shared rooms, care tasks, med passes and group activities. It suggests who to notify and which staff to monitor.
  • Screening: a kiosk-mode tablet at the entrance for visitors, and in-app screening before staff can clock in. A failed screen blocks the clock-in and notifies the manager.
  • Hand hygiene audits: a quick observation form (5 moments, role, outcome) feeds the compliance KPI and the facility heat map.

Layout zones

  1. Report library. Grouped as operational, clinical, survey and compliance, and the user's own reports. Org admins can publish reports to every facility, or restrict them to certain roles.
  2. Builder. Filter chips for date range, facility, resident, staff, diagnosis and drug class, plus grouping and chart type. The preview updates live, and the matching table sits below the chart.
  3. Scheduling. Frequency, format (PDF, Excel, CSV), recipients and delivery method. Email always carries a link that requires sign-in, never a file containing health information.
  4. Activity log. Who ran, viewed, exported or received this report. The full history lives in the organization audit log.

Survey readiness

  • State survey packet: one click assembles census, MAR exceptions, incident logs, training records, fire drill logs, infection logs and care plan reviews for a chosen period. The packet is bookmarked and watermarked.
  • Auditor access: a time-limited, read-only role scoped to chosen facilities and dates. It is logged in detail and expires automatically.
  • Executive scorecards: KPI cards, trends, heat maps and ranked facilities, ready to present or export as a board deck PDF.
  • Large exports run in the background with a progress toast. Files are encrypted, expire after 7 days, and carry the user's name and time in the footer.
Behavior

Key user flows

Each flow lists the steps in order, the actor, and what the system does. Steps marked as a hard stop cannot be skipped.

Organization & facility onboarding

Org owner · 20–40 min
  1. Create organization. Legal name, timezone, care types (IDD, senior, behavioral). Sign the BAA electronically. Owner sets up MFA, which is required.
  2. Choose plan. Pricing per facility and per licensed bed. The estimate updates as homes are added. Large clients can enable white-label (logo, colors, custom domain).
  3. Add facility. Address, license number and expiry, licensed bed count, and the geofence radius for the time clock, drawn on a map.
  4. Build floor plan. Drag rooms onto a grid, name them, and set beds per room. Copy the layout from another facility if it matches.
  5. Bring in people. Upload staff and residents from CSV or the previous system's export. A mapping screen matches columns, then staff are invited by SMS or email.
  6. Apply templates. Choose org care plans, forms, schedules and policies to copy into the facility.
  7. Go-live checklist. MAR imported and RN-verified, first schedule published, devices registered. A progress bar shows readiness, and the facility goes live when everything is checked.
Result: the facility appears on the Organization Dashboard and billing updates for the next cycle.

Resident intake & admission

Manager + RN · tablet
  1. Start intake from Residents → Admit, or turn a pending referral into a resident. A duplicate check runs on name, date of birth and Medicaid ID.
  2. Demographics and photo. Take a photo with the camera. Scan the ID and insurance card, and OCR fills in the fields.
  3. Clinical. Diagnoses (ICD-10 search), allergies (coded, with severity), diet and swallow level, behavioral notes, physician and pharmacy.
  4. Legal and contacts. Guardianship documents, emergency contacts, consent forms. E-signatures captured on screen or sent to the guardian by secure link.
  5. Acuity scoring. A guided assessment produces a care level of 1–4 and suggests a care plan template and staffing ratio.
  6. Assign room on the room map. The admission checklist (TB test, physical, med reconciliation) creates tasks with due dates.
Result: resident profile goes live. A draft care plan goes to the RN for approval. Census and billing update.

Scheduled med pass

Med-certified DSP · phone
  1. Open eMAR → 08:00 pass. Residents are sorted by room, with isolated residents last. Allergies and swallow level appear on each card.
  2. Verify the resident. Scan the wristband or room QR and confirm the photo. A mismatch is a hard stop.
  3. Scan each med. The system checks drug, dose, route, time window, allergies and duplicates. Any failure is a hard stop and requires an RN override with a reason.
  4. Pre-checks. Vitals or blood sugar requested by the order. A value outside the order's limits suggests a hold.
  5. Confirm administration. One button signs all scanned meds. Refused or held meds need a reason and notify the RN.
  6. Pass complete. A summary shows any exceptions. Anything not given by the end of the window becomes a missed-dose alert.
Result: MAR updated, compliance metrics recalculated, and pharmacy refills counted down.

PRN or controlled substance

DSP + witness · phone
  1. Quick action → Log Med → PRN, or from the resident's page. Only PRN meds that are allowed right now are listed. Locked meds show when they unlock next.
  2. Choose a reason from the order's indications, plus a pain or symptom score where applicable.
  3. Scan the med. For a controlled med, the count before and after is shown.
  4. Witness signs. A second user enters their PIN on the same device. The witness must be a different person with the right credential.
  5. Effectiveness check scheduled. A reminder fires at the configured interval (default 60 min) to whoever is assigned to the resident.
  6. Rate effectiveness. Effective, partly effective or not effective. If not effective, the RN is notified and a note prompt appears.
Result: controlled count updated. A count discrepancy at shift change creates an incident automatically.

Start an isolation order

RN or IP · any device
  1. Quick action → Start Isolation, or Resident → Infection → New order. A symptom picker suggests the precaution type based on CDC guidance.
  2. Set the order. Type (Contact, Droplet, Airborne, Enhanced barrier, or combined), suspected organism, start time and review interval.
  3. Link or create a case with onset date, symptoms and labs ordered.
  4. Spread the order. Badge added to the profile, room tile, eMAR and task rows. Task checklists gain PPE steps. A door sign is ready to print.
  5. Exposure review. A 72-hour contact list is built from care tasks, roommates and activities. Confirm who to notify or monitor.
  6. Review and clear. When the review is due, the RN extends or discontinues the order with criteria met. Clearing removes the badges and triggers a terminal room clean.
Result: the order counts on the Infection Board, and the review clock is tracked until it clears.

Outbreak declaration

Infection preventionist
  1. Threshold alert. The system flags a possible cluster, for example 2 or more residents with similar symptoms in 72 hours in one facility.
  2. Review cluster. The IP sees the line list and epi curve and either dismisses the alert with a reason or starts a declaration.
  3. Declare. Pick the organism and affected area. A checklist template for that organism loads.
  4. Response actions. Cohorting suggestions for rooms and staff, a stepped-up cleaning schedule with the right disinfectant, paused admissions and group activities, visitor restrictions.
  5. Notify. Health department report generated, families and guardians messaged through the portal, staff alerted. Each is logged as done.
  6. Monitor daily. New cases, staff off work, PPE burn rate. The situation banner shows at the facility.
  7. Close. Available after two incubation periods with no new onset. Generates an after-action report.
Result: a complete outbreak record for surveyors and public health, with a timestamp on every step.

Incident report & investigation

Any staff → manager
  1. Quick action → Report Incident. Pick the type (fall, behavior, injury, med error, elopement, property) and the resident(s) and staff involved.
  2. Describe. What happened, where and when, with photos and immediate actions. Voice-to-text available. Takes about 2 minutes.
  3. Severity triage. High severity (injury, abuse allegation, hospital transfer) pages the manager and RN at once and starts the reporting clock for state notification.
  4. Investigation. The manager assigns an investigator. A root-cause template, witness statements and a timeline are added.
  5. Follow-up. Corrective actions with owners and due dates, care plan updates, and staff supervision notes if needed.
  6. Close and notify. Guardian notification logged. The state report is generated if required.
Result: the incident is linked to the resident, staff and facility records and feeds the incident KPIs and heat map.

Clock in, handoff, clock out

Floor staff · phone
  1. Clock in. The geofence confirms the user is on site (optional per facility). The health screening must pass first.
  2. Read handoff. The previous shift's note and any open items appear. Tapping to confirm records that it was read.
  3. Work the shift. Today lists assigned residents and tasks. The manager sees workload live and can reassign by dragging.
  4. Write handoff. The draft is pre-filled with exceptions: refused meds, incidents, new isolation, PRNs given. Staff add free text.
  5. Clock out. Blocked until required items are done or handed off (for example, a controlled count). Overtime warnings appear at the 8- and 12-hour marks.
Result: timesheet ready for payroll export and the handoff passed to the next shift.

Push a template to facilities

Org admin · desktop
  1. Edit in Templates. For example, update the Fall Prevention care plan to v3. Changes are tracked against v2.
  2. Choose targets. Pick facilities and choose a mode: required (locked fields), recommended (facility can accept or adjust), or new residents only.
  3. Preview impact. Shows how many active care plans use v2 and which facilities have local changes that would conflict.
  4. Publish. Facility managers get a review task. Required changes apply on the effective date.
  5. Track adoption. A status grid shows each facility as adopted, pending or adjusted, with the reason for any local adjustment.
Result: consistent standards across homes, with version history for surveyors.

Family & guardian portal access

Manager → guardian
  1. Invite. From Resident → Contacts. The relationship and legal authority are checked against documents on file.
  2. Set visibility. Choose what the guardian can see: daily summary, appointments, selected documents, photos, incident notifications. Medical details are hidden by default.
  3. Guardian joins. Identity is verified by a one-time code to a phone number on file, then they set up MFA and accept the portal terms.
  4. Use the portal. Read-only updates, document downloads, secure messages to the house team, and e-signatures for consents.
Result: every view and download is recorded in the resident's access log.

Trust

Security, tenancy & HIPAA in the interface

Most security work happens in the architecture. The items below are the parts users see and touch.

Tenant isolation

  • Each organization signs in on its own subdomain or white-label domain, with its branding on the sign-in page.
  • Every record carries a tenant ID, enforced by database row-level security, and each tenant has its own encryption key.
  • Support staff can't see tenant data unless the client grants time-limited access, which the client can watch in the audit log.
  • Search, exports and integrations are scoped to one tenant at the API layer, and cross-tenant tests run in CI.

Sign-in & sessions

  • SSO (SAML or OIDC) for enterprise clients. MFA required for admins and anyone who can export.
  • Shared floor devices: a full sign-in at the start of the shift, then a 4–6 digit PIN to resume after the screen locks (default 5 minutes).
  • The lock screen and app switcher hide all health information.
  • Devices are registered per facility and can be wiped remotely.

Minimum necessary & break-the-glass

  • Lists show only the fields the role needs. Sensitive items such as behavioral health and HIV status are hidden until revealed with one tap, and each reveal is logged.
  • Opening a resident outside the user's assignment asks for a reason (emergency, covering a shift) and alerts the privacy officer.
  • Exports carry a watermark with the user's name and time.

Audit trail

  • Append-only log of every view, create, update, delete, print, export and permission change, with the user, device, IP, facility and before/after values.
  • Shown on each record as a History drawer, and organization-wide in Settings → Audit with filters and export.
  • Retained for at least 6 years, matching HIPAA documentation retention.
Field conditions

Mobile & offline

Group homes often have weak Wi-Fi in back bedrooms and basements, so floor workflows must keep working when the connection drops.

What works offline

Med pass (scheduled and PRN), care tasks, notes, vitals, incident drafts, screening, clock in and out (with device time and a GPS fix), and reading handoff notes. Each facility's shift data is cached encrypted on the device at clock-in.

What stays online only

Controlled-substance counts at shift change, isolation order changes, outbreak declarations, care plan approvals, and exports. These need the latest data from the server so two devices can't conflict.

Sync & conflicts

A header chip shows Online, Offline, or how many items are waiting to sync. Records sync in order with device timestamps. If two people gave the same dose offline, a duplicate-dose conflict is flagged to the RN and is never merged silently.

Device patterns

Phone: bottom nav, quick-action button, swipe actions, camera barcode scanning. Tablet: two-pane views (list and detail), works with a Bluetooth scanner. Desktop: left sidebar, data-dense grids, keyboard shortcuts.

Floor mode

A per-user setting that raises the base font to 18px, uses 48px targets, simplifies the Today view, and turns on glove-friendly large buttons for confirming meds.

Performance targets

Today screen interactive in under 2 seconds on a mid-range Android over 3G. A scan confirms in under 300 ms. Offline actions save locally in under 100 ms.
